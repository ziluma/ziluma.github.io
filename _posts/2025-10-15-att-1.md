[Attention is all you need](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762). We go over the original multi-head attention. I learned this based on Andrej Karpathy's wonderful [videos](https://youtu.be/l8pRSuU81PU?si=6VVU7gPJlmU81AVF).

For a matrix $\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n},\mathbf{A}_i$ denotes the $i^{\rm th}$ row of $\mathbf{A}.$

For simplicity, we work on matrices of the same size.

For query $\mathbf{Q}$, key $\mathbf{K}$, and value $\mathbf{V}\in \mathbb{R}^{T\times d}$, the single-head self-attention is

$$
    \mathrm{Att}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{K},\mathbf{V})
    := \mathcal{S}\circ \mathcal{M}\left(d^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{Q} \mathbf{K}^\top\right)\mathbf{V},
$$

where the softmax $\mathcal{S}:\mathbb{R}^m\to \mathbb{R}^m$ is defined as
$$
\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{z})_i = \frac{\mathrm{e}^{z_i}}{\sum_j \mathrm{e}^{z_j}}.
$$
$\mathcal{M}$ is some mask satisfying $\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{Z})_{ts}=\mathbf{Z}_{ts}$ or $-\infty$ for $\mathbf{Z}\in \mathbb{R}^{T\times T}.$ 


For a hidden input $\mathbf{H}\in \mathbb{R}^{T\times D}$ with $D=H\cdot d,$ where $H$ is the number of heads, let

$$
    \mathbf{Q}^{(h)}:=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{W}^{{\rm Q}(h)},\quad
    \mathbf{K}^{(h)}:=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{W}^{{\rm K}(h)},\quad
    \mathbf{V}^{(h)}:=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{W}^{{\rm V}(h)},
$$

where $\mathbf{W}^{{\rm Q}(h)},\mathbf{W}^{{\rm K}(h)},\mathbf{W}^{{\rm V}(h)}\in \mathbb{R}^{D\times d}.$
The multi-head attention (MHA) is defined as

$$
{\rm MHA}(\mathbf{H}) := [\mathbf{A}^{(1)} \cdots\mathbf{A}^{(H)}]\mathbf{W}^{\rm O},
$$
where 

$$
    \mathbf{A}^{(h)} := \mathrm{Att}\left(\mathbf{Q}^{(h)}, \mathbf{K}^{(h)}, \mathbf{V}^{(h)}\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{T\times d},
$$

 and $\mathbf{W}^{\rm O}\in \mathbb{R}^{D\times D}$. If

$$
\mathbf{P}^{(h)} := \mathcal{S}\circ \mathcal{M}\left(d^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{Q}^{(h)} \mathbf{K}^{(h)\top}\right),
$$

then

$$
    \mathbf{A}^{(h)}_t = \sum_{s} \mathbf{P}_{ts}^{(h)} \mathbf{V}^{(h)}_s,
$$
where recall $\mathbf{A}_t$ denotes the $t^{\rm th}$ row of $\mathbf{A}.$ $\mathbf{A}_t^{(h)}$ is a weighted sum of values.

In fact, if we write 

$$
\begin{align*}
\mathbf{W}^{\rm O} = \begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{W}^{(1)\top} \\ \vdots\\ \mathbf{W}^{(H)\top}
\end{bmatrix}
\end{align*}
$$

where $\mathbf{W}^{(h)}\in \mathbb{R}^{D\times d},$ then

$$
\begin{align*}
{\rm MHA}(\mathbf{H})
&= \sum_{h=1}^H \mathbf{A}^{(h)} \mathbf{W}^{(h)\top}
= \sum_{h=1}^H \mathbf{P}^{(h)}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{W}^{{\rm V}(h)}\mathbf{W}^{(h)\top}.
\end{align*}
$$

In the sum, $\mathbf{W}^{{\rm V}(h)}\mathbf{W}^{(h)\top}$ has **rank $\le d$**, which can be viewed as compression in each head, providing a special viewpoint while saving memories.

Let me mention a more or less trivial observation.
If the weights/probabilities $\mathbf{P}^{(h)}$'s are almost the same $\approx \mathbf{P}$, then 

$$
\begin{align*}
{\rm MHA}(\mathbf{H})
&\approx
\mathbf{P} \mathbf{H} \sum_{h=1}^H \mathbf{W}^{{\rm V}(h)}\mathbf{W}^{(h)\top}.
\end{align*}
$$

Now the sum can be a full-rank matrix without any restriction, and $\mathbf{W}^{\rm O}$ is actually redundant. 
This is probably when the data is too small for the model and of course there's no need for multi-heads.
